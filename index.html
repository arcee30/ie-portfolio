My Story

I am from Princeton, New Jersey, and I came to Duke because it offered both a top-tier education and a strong social culture through sports and community. At the time, Duke felt partly like fate: it was the best school I had been accepted to, and I still had a lot to learn about what I wanted my college experience to become.

I am pursuing a B.S. in Computer Science with an AI/ML concentration, the Innovation & Entrepreneurship Certificate, and a Master of Engineering in AI for Product Innovation (AIPI) through a 4+0.5 plan. I have been interested in computer science since fifth grade. As AI became more central to technology, my focus moved from building websites and games toward impactful AI applications using LLMs, retrieval-augmented generation, and applied product development.

03

Genmab

Enterprise generative AI in biotechnology

I completed a summer internship at Genmab, a biotechnology company focused on developing medicines through science, data, and innovation. I worked with the Data Science and AI Products team on enterprise generative AI projects, including prototypes using ChatGPT, OpenAI APIs, retrieval-augmented generation, data visualization, and product development.

My role centered on building and improving document intelligence tools. I worked with OpenAI APIs, vector embeddings, Amazon Bedrock, Streamlit, Flask, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript while improving usability, supported file types, error handling, and runtime performance through caching.

Why I chose it I wanted hands-on exposure to how AI is used inside a real biotechnology company, not just in a classroom or personal project.
Innovative ideas I helped turn large-document search, summarization, and knowledge access into practical generative AI workflows for internal users.
Failure and lesson Early prototypes showed that a technically impressive demo could still fail if retrieval quality, runtime, or error handling made it hard to trust. I learned to treat usability and reliability as part of the core product.
Most important learning Successful enterprise AI depends on stakeholder needs, clear communication, iteration, and careful translation of technical capabilities into useful tools.

Reflection

This experience showed me how emerging AI capabilities become useful products only when they are shaped around real users, stakeholder needs, communication, iteration, and usability.

04

Uncharted

AI workflows for institutional investment research

I worked with Uncharted, an AI startup building Alloy, a product for institutional investors. Alloy helps investment teams improve research workflows by combining AI, structured investment intelligence, provenance tracking, and firm-specific knowledge.

My role focused on AI engineering, web development, and agentic AI workflows for investment memo generation, peer selection, and analysis of public-company guidance versus actual performance. I also demoed work to clients, incorporated feedback, and helped lead another student on a critical build.

Why I chose it I wanted to work in a startup environment where I could build real AI products, move quickly, and connect technical work to customer value.
Innovative ideas I worked on AI-driven investment memo generation, peer selection, public-company guidance analysis, validation checks, and user-facing output design.
Failure and lesson Some AI outputs were too generic or not grounded enough for investment research. I learned that credible AI products need provenance, structure, validation, and feedback loops before users can trust them.
Most important learning I learned how entrepreneurial teams test customer pain points, prioritize under uncertainty, demo to clients, and turn differentiated technology into a venture.

Reflection

Uncharted helped me see how an entrepreneurial team turns a customer pain point into a product, tests traction with paying customers, and builds a business around differentiated technology.
